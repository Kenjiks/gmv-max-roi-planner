How this works

Contribution margin = what's left of each 1 of GMV after COGS, platform fees, affiliate commission, refunds and per-order shipping/packaging, before ads.

Break-even ROI = 1 ÷ contribution margin. Below this ROI, every sale loses money once ad spend is counted.

Scaling model: GMV ∝ (target ROI)−elasticity, anchored on the current campaign. A lower ROI target lets GMV Max bid for a wider audience, so GMV rises, but ad spend rises faster.

Sweet-spot ROI = the ROI that maximises net profit = break-even ROI × (1 + 1/elasticity).

Guarantee floor: GMV Max credits back the shortfall if a day delivers less than 90% of target ROI. The worst case is about 0.9 × target ROI, and the "worst-case profit" column assumes that.

Caveats: this is a planning model, not a forecast. It is most reliable within about ±30% of the ROI the campaign has actually run at. Real results also depend on budget caps, creative quality, stock, and seasonality. Test ROI changes step by step (e.g. −10 to −15% every few days) and recalibrate with real results.
